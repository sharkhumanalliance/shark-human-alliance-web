# Approved Shark Facts And Sources

Last updated: 2026-05-31

Use this file for every factual post. If a number is not here, do not publish it until it is added with a citation.

## Source-Line Format

Use source lines inside graphics, not only in captions.

Preferred visual source line:

`SOURCE - WORM ET AL. 2013 - DULVY ET AL. 2021 - FLORIDA MUSEUM ISAF`

Use `SOURCE` rather than `SOURCES` if the line is short and design needs it.

For single-fact posts, use one source:

`SOURCE - WORM ET AL., MARINE POLICY, 2013`

## Fact 1: Human-Caused Shark Mortality

### Approved Short Wording

Humans kill an estimated 100 million sharks per year.

### Approved Precise Wording

Humans kill an estimated 100 million sharks per year, with a published range of 63-273 million.

### Bureau Wording

For the record: humans kill an estimated 100 million sharks per year. The published range is 63-273 million. The Bureau finds the paperwork inadequate.

### Citation

Worm, B. et al. (2013). "Global catches, exploitation rates, and rebuilding options for sharks." Marine Policy, 40, 194-204. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpol.2012.12.034

### Notes

Use "estimated" every time.

Do not write "exactly 100 million".

Do not write "100 million sharks are murdered" in factual posts. Keep the number precise and credible.

## Fact 2: Extinction Risk

### Approved Short Wording

More than one-third of shark, ray, and chimaera species are threatened with extinction.

### Approved Precise Wording

Dulvy et al. assessed 1,199 shark, ray, and chimaera species and found 391, or 32.6%, in IUCN threatened categories.

### Bureau Wording

More than one-third of shark, ray, and chimaera species are threatened with extinction. The Department regrets the size of the fraction.

### Citation

Dulvy, N. K. et al. (2021). "Overfishing drives over one-third of all sharks and rays toward a global extinction crisis." Current Biology, 31, 4773-4787.e8. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.08.062

### Notes

Prefer "shark, ray, and chimaera species" or "sharks and rays" depending on space.

Avoid "one-third of all shark species" because the cited assessment covers the broader chondrichthyan group.

## Fact 3: Human Fatalities From Unprovoked Shark Bites

### Approved Short Wording

Fewer than 10 human fatalities from unprovoked shark bites occur in most years.

### Approved Precise Wording

The Florida Museum International Shark Attack File reported a recent five-year annual global average of eight unprovoked fatalities per year in its 2025 summary.

### Bureau Wording

Fewer than 10 human fatalities from unprovoked shark bites occur in most years. The Bureau notes that the public relations imbalance remains severe.

### Citation

Florida Museum of Natural History, International Shark Attack File, "Yearly Worldwide Shark Attack Summary." https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/shark-attacks/yearly-worldwide-summary/

### Notes

Always say "unprovoked shark bites" or "unprovoked shark bite fatalities" when using ISAF figures.

Do not compare to "sharks kill fewer than 10 humans" without context. That wording is punchy but less precise.

Verify before using the specific "eight" average in new graphics. ISAF publishes annual summaries, so this value should be refreshed yearly. The evergreen wording "fewer than 10 human fatalities from unprovoked shark bites occur in most years" is safer for reusable templates.

## Fact 4: Shark Evolution Timeline

### Approved Short Wording

Sharks and their ancient relatives have a fossil record stretching back about 450 million years.

### Approved Precise Wording

The earliest fossil evidence for sharks or their ancestors includes scales dating to about 450 million years ago.

### Bureau Wording

Sharks have paperwork older than trees, if fossil scales count as paperwork. The Bureau has chosen to count them.

### Citation

Natural History Museum, "Shark evolution: a 450 million year timeline." https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/shark-evolution-a-450-million-year-timeline.html

### Notes

Use "sharks or their ancestors" when being precise.

Do not imply modern shark species have been unchanged for 450 million years.
