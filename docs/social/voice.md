# Social Voice Guide

Last updated: 2026-06-05

## Voice Formula

Humor first - cute character comedy and dry Bureau bureaucracy, both welcome - plus real conservation.

Finnley and Luna are expressive, lovable characters who treat absurd human prejudice as a deadly serious administrative emergency. Lead with humor in whichever register fits the moment: warm and cute when the characters carry the post, dry and bureaucratic when the Bureau is filing the joke straight. Both registers belong to the same brand; neither owns it. The goal is to be funny enough to earn the audience - dryness alone or cuteness alone is not the point.

## Brand Voice Rules

Sound official but lovable. Cute and funny beats flat and dry. Big character emotion is welcome - Finnley can be dramatic, even tearful on a bad day; Luna stays deadpan, which is the contrast. Let the absurdity play; do not explain the joke. Keep facts precise and source-backed (see `sources.md`). Keep product mentions in-world (file paperwork, close the case).

## Sentence Shape

Good:

- "The Bureau has reviewed the situation."
- "No timely objection has been received."
- "This has been filed for the public record."
- "Luna has initialed the document without further comment."

Avoid:

- "We are so excited to announce..."
- "Tag your bestie if they need this!"
- "You won't believe this shark fact!"
- "Let's save sharks together!"

## Caption Rules

- Emoji allowed but sparingly; let the characters and visuals carry the cuteness, not an emoji wall.
- Exclamation marks are fine for character emotion (Finnley especially). Do not pepper every line.
- No explaining the joke after the joke.
- No guilt-heavy activism language.
- No clickbait or fake urgency.
- First line / first frame must work on its own.
- If the post is factual, the caption should not overstate the graphic.
- Comment-bait CTAs are welcome when they stay in-world (e.g. "Comment ACQUITTED to support the defendant", "Guilty or misunderstood?").

## Tagging and Comment CTAs

Tagging and verdict-style comment CTAs are encouraged - they are how the Wanted/case mechanic spreads. Keep them characterful rather than generic: prefer "Open a case on the human who needs Lesson 1" or "Comment ACQUITTED to support the defendant" over a flat "Tag a friend who". The mechanic and the cuteness do the work.

## Comment And Engagement Rules

Reply in-character to playful comments only.

Use plain human tone for genuine criticism, genuine distress, conservation questions, refund/support issues, or anything involving real injuries. Never argue, never punch down, and never use Bureau jokes when someone is upset.

If a comment relates to a real shark incident, follow [crisis.md](./crisis.md).

## Forbidden Phrases

Do not use:

- "Hey guys"
- "You won't believe"
- "Let's make a change"
- "Join the movement"
- "Limited-time offer"
- "Buy now"
- "This is hilarious"
- "We made this funny thing"
- "Ocean vibes"
- "Shark attack season"

Also avoid fear-based hooks:

- "Terrified of sharks?"
- "Don't get eaten"
- "Avoid shark attacks"

Never imply the product provides real safety.

## Approved Voice Patterns

### Bureau Memo

`Memo {number} - {officially absurd statement}.`

Example:

`Memo 0001 - The Bureau is operational. Filing has commenced.`

### Public Record

`Filed for the public record: {fact}.`

Example:

`Filed for the public record: sharks have had better centuries.`

### Luna

`Luna reviewed this. {dry outcome}.`

Examples:

- `Luna reviewed this. Six lines did not survive.`
- `Luna has initialed the document without visible enthusiasm.`
- `Forwarded to Luna. The Bureau regrets the delay.`

### Finnley

`Finnley has prepared a statement. {small caveat}.`

Examples:

- `Finnley has prepared a statement. He insists it is brief.`
- `Finnley remains optimistic. This is not legally binding.`
- `Finnley has reviewed the footage and blames cinema.`

### Reusable Finnley Statements

Public-record statements attributable to Finnley as press spokesperson. Use as pull-quotes in graphics, FB caption highlights, or comment replies. Sparingly: one per post, and do not contradict a filed statement in later posts.

- `The Bureau wishes to clarify that the 1975 film was not, in fact, a documentary.`
- `The Bureau has reviewed the footage. Sharks are not, as suggested, plotting.`
- `Hollywood has been notified, in writing, that sharks are not auditioning for the role of antagonist.`
- `Statistically, the average shark is having a quiet day.`
- `Most humans entering the ocean are not, technically, on the menu.`
- `The Bureau remains optimistic that shark-human relations can be improved administratively.`
- `The Bureau cannot speak for all sharks. No shark has so far objected.`

First on the record (relaunch post R1.1, Emergency PR Meeting — the originally planned static post 1.3 was never published): `The Bureau wishes to clarify that the 1975 film was not, in fact, a documentary.`

### Wanted

`A case has been opened regarding {person/status}.`

Examples:

- `A case has been opened regarding an unprotected human.`
- `Subject appears confident. Documentation remains absent.`
- `The Bureau recommends immediate filing.`

### Product Bridge

`Close the case with paperwork.`

Examples:

- `If the accusation stands, paperwork may now be filed.`
- `The case may be resolved through Form FIN-1.`
- `Protection status can be improved administratively.`

2. `No shark has filed a timely objection.`

4. `Finnley has prepared a statement. Luna has shortened it.`

7. `Subject appears confident. Documentation remains absent.`

10. `A small procedural improvement has occurred.`

14. `Finnley remains optimistic about shark-human relations.`
15. `The Department of Misunderstanding Prevention has been notified.`
16. `The Bureau finds the ratio administratively embarrassing.`
17. `The case remains open pending documentation.`
18. `Public filing will be available shortly.`
19. `A certificate may resolve this matter aesthetically.`

Use `allegedly` sparingly. It should feel like a dry legal tic, not a catchphrase in every post.
